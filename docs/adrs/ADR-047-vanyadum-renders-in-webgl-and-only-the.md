---

«ВАНЯДУМ» is a first-person shooter, and it draws its world with **three.js over WebGL2**, in a `<canvas>`, loaded as an async route chunk. **[ADR-046](./ADR-046-the-shared-plane-is-dom-and-css-never-a.md) is not reversed by this**: that record governs «Ванягоччи»'s shared plane, which stays DOM and CSS, and it names its own escape hatch — *"a hand-rolled `<canvas>` leaf component inside one part of the screen, with the DOM still authoritative for layout, state and every interactive element"*. This is that shape, and every trigger ADR-046 wrote down for re-asking the question is hit at once: a camera over a world far larger than the viewport, smooth per-frame motion rather than 5 Hz frames plus CSS transitions, and a scene graph that genuinely exists.

**The line, and it is drawn hard: the canvas holds the world and nothing else.** The heads-up display, the movement stick, the fire button, the splash screen, the rules cheatsheet, the connection notice and the result screen are all ordinary DOM. That is a **testing** decision before it is a design one, and it is the direct consequence of taking ADR-046's strongest argument seriously rather than waving it away.

_What the canvas costs, stated plainly._ The yard enjoys several hundred end-to-end assertions that describe it through real layout — stacking resolved with `document.elementFromPoint`, feet on the ground through every pose, tap targets, label caps, reduced motion genuinely applying. **None of that is available inside a canvas.** The only assertions left would be pixel comparisons or a test-only introspection API, and shipping a test hook into a production path is forbidden here (`CLAUDE.md` → *Production Code Stays Production-Only*), so it would be pixel comparisons — the tests that rot and then get deleted. This bears directly on [ADR-021](./ADR-021-two-playwright-suites-on-purpose.md), because a canvas blinds both Playwright suites at once.

_So the response is to move everything a test needs OUT of it, in two directions._

- **Downwards, into pure functions.** The level's geometry is built by `buildLevelMeshes`, which turns the server's sector graph into plain arrays of numbers and imports nothing from three.js. The textures are generated by `generateTexture`, a `(surface, size, seed) → Uint8Array` — deliberately **not** drawn into a 2D canvas context, which would be untestable in jsdom and unassertable anywhere. The input maths, the command batching and the rules cheatsheet are the same shape. All of it runs in node under vitest, and the renderer is reduced to a thin layer that uploads what those produced.
- **Sideways, into the DOM.** Everything a person reads or touches stays real: `web/e2e/gamevanyadum.spec.ts` asserts the cheatsheet's derived numbers, the HUD following a snapshot, 44-pixel targets, `touch-action: none` on the play surface, the result screen, and no horizontal overflow at 360 px. A HUD painted into the canvas would have been marginally prettier and would have deleted all of it.

_Why three.js, and not the alternatives._ PixiJS is 2D, and a raycaster cannot produce the non-flat level the game needs. A hand-rolled WebGL renderer is precisely the failure ADR-046 names as its own tripwire — *"writing an engine badly instead of importing one well"* — and this game needs a camera, a projection, materials and a depth buffer on day one. A Godot or Unity WASM export is tens of megabytes to an audience holding phones on mobile data. Babylon.js is larger than three for capabilities this game does not use.

_The bundle, measured rather than argued._ Tree-shaken three.js is **176.7 kB gzip** in its own chunk; the view and the scene module add 8.7 kB gzip between them. Against the app's entry bundle of 98.3 kB gzip of JavaScript, that is substantial — and it is paid **once, by a person who opens «ВАНЯДУМ»**, under a content-hashed filename the browser caches, and by nobody else. The route is lazy and `vanyadumScene` is imported dynamically inside it, so the engine is not in the entry graph at all. The budget for iteration 1 was 200 kB gzip and the measured total is 185.4 kB. Anything that pushes past it gets trimmed — explicit imports, no `examples/jsm` helpers, our own controls — rather than having the budget raised.

_And a real path, not a test hook._ WebGL can be disabled by policy, blocked by a driver, or refused by a phone saving power, and a browser that passes the availability probe can still fail to create a context when it is at its limit. Both cases are handled in production: the splash explains that this browser cannot run the game and points at the other sections, and a context that fails mid-start leaves the HUD and the way out on screen rather than a black rectangle with no button on it. That a CI run can therefore assert on the HUD with no GPU is a happy consequence, not the reason.

_What would change this decision._ Written as triggers, so a future reader can check them rather than re-argue the whole thing:

- **Anything textual or interactive moving onto the canvas.** This is the one that matters, and it is a regression rather than an evolution: it would silently delete the test surface this record exists to protect.
- **The bundle budget being exceeded** without the trimming above recovering it.
- **The engine turning out to be the hard part.** Nothing about iteration 1 was made difficult by three.js; the difficulties were the netcode's shape and thumbs on glass. If that reverses, a smaller hand-rolled renderer becomes worth costing again.
