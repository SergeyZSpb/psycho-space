---

Every day at **21:00 UTC — midnight in Moscow, where everybody who plays this lives** — the office in «СИМУЛЯТОР ФИНТЕХА» is rebuilt, and every shift standing in it ends with the «РЕМОНТ» ending that [ADR-061](./ADR-061-the-office-floor-is-validated-data-not-a.md)'s iteration 3 already built for an admin pressing a button. It is a **rule of the game** rather than a maintenance window, which is why it is stated on the splash screen next to the walk speed and the board window, and why the hour rides the served catalogue rather than being typed into the browser.

_The day's office is a pure function of the day, and that is the whole decision._ A renovation day is an integer — how many whole days have passed since the epoch, bucketed on 21:00 rather than on midnight — and the floor for it is `Generate(seedForDay(day))`. **No floor is written because time passed**, nothing is read to find out what today's office is, and two processes running the same binary on the same date are standing in the same room without having agreed on anything.

_Said exactly, because the loose version is not true._ A rotation **does** write — once per occupant — because it *ends their shifts*, and an ended shift is recorded here exactly as one the лысый ended is, through the same buffered channel and the same single writer goroutine. What never happens is a row appearing to say what the office looks like today. With that distinction held, **two claims this project makes elsewhere stay literally true rather than nearly true**: [ADR-038](./ADR-038-time-varying-state-is-computed-on-read-never.md)'s "time-varying state is computed on read, never ticked" — the day's floor is a closed form over the clock, exactly as a pet's hunger is — and `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s "no Redis, no cron, no worker and no queue", since this added no goroutine at all. A third is borrowed rather than preserved: [ADR-034](./ADR-034-the-broadcast-tick-is-injected-and-belongs-to.md)'s tick is a *parameter*, which is what lets "it is now one second past nine in the evening" be something a test says rather than something it waits a day for.

_The alternative was a stored row, and it is worse in a way that only shows up later._ Writing the day's floor at 21:00 means a table whose contents depend on whether a process happened to be running at 21:00 — so a night of downtime leaves a hole, a deploy that overlaps the boundary races two processes to insert, and "which floor is today's" becomes a question you answer by reading rows rather than by evaluating an expression. It also invites a **catch-up** concept — the process was down for three days, do we rebuild three times? — which is a genuinely hard question with no good answer, and which **the derived version does not have**, because a day's office was never an event in a queue. There is exactly one thing to know, it is `renovationDay(now)`, and everything follows from it.

_Why the tick is the reader, rather than a timer or a lazy read._ Three candidates, and two of them are wrong for reasons worth writing down.

- **A lazy read**, the way a pet's death is materialised by whoever observes it first ([ADR-038](./ADR-038-time-varying-state-is-computed-on-read-never.md)). It cannot work here because **a rotation is destructive**: it ends *other people's* shifts. "The first person to open the splash screen throws everybody else out" is a rule with a person's identity in it, and until somebody looked, a shift would go on earning money on a floor that no longer exists. A pet's death harms nobody but the pet.
- **A goroutine with a timer.** It would work. It is also a fourth thing in this process owning a clock, with its own shutdown to get right, its own test seam, and its own failure mode when it dies quietly and nobody notices for a week.
- **The simulation loop**, which won. It already runs at 20 Hz, already holds the mutex that owns the floor, and already knows how to empty a room and tell everybody why — so it compares two integers as its first act on every tick, and the entire feature is `if renovationDay(now) > s.installedDay`. `installedDay` is the *only* state the rotation has, it is not durable, and all it means is "this process has already dealt with that boundary".

_It is the **first** statement of `step`, above the empty-office return, and that placement is load-bearing._ The plan the tick rotates is the same plan `GET /config` serves, and a splash screen sitting on a phone at ten past nine has to be shown the office the next shift will actually open on. A rotation that only fired when somebody was inside would hand the first person to clock in a floor their cached catalogue does not describe — the exact staleness `office_id` exists to prevent, arrived at from the other direction.

_The guard is `>` and not `!=`, which is the backwards-clock guard._ An NTP correction or a resumed virtual machine can hand the loop an instant **earlier** than the one before it. Read as "a different day", that evicts everybody onto yesterday's floor and then evicts them again when the clock catches up — two mass evictions for one clock error. Comparing for "later than the day in force" makes a clock that goes backwards a **no-op**: the office keeps the newer floor, which is the one every client is already drawing.

_A restart, a deploy overlap and a missed 21:00 all produce the same room._ The boot path runs the identical arithmetic. `EnsureLayout` treats a stored row as an **override** of the day's own office — the starting floor a fresh database installs for itself, or a floor an admin rebuilt — and it is in force **only while `renovationDay(created_at) == renovationDay(now)`**. Anything older lost to a 21:00 that has passed since, so the floor is `dailyLayout(today)`, derived and unwritten, reported with `SourceDaily` and with the day's own 21:00 as its installation time. A process starting at 22:00 therefore computes exactly what a process that ran through 21:00 computed; two processes overlapping during a deploy agree rather than race; and three days of downtime install today's office rather than four in a row. `SourceDaily` is a separate source from `SourceGenerated` for one reason: both come out of the same generator, but one is what the building does every night and the other is somebody having pressed a button since — and "is an override in force?" is the first question an admin asks the control room.

_A failed draw records the day and keeps the floor._ `Generate` can refuse a seed that produced too thin a floor. No day in a five-year sweep does (`TestEveryDailyOfficeForFiveYearsIsPlayable`), but the honest answer to one that did is: keep the office in force, log it, and **mark the day as dealt with anyway** — because `dailyLayout` is a pure function of the day, an attempt that failed cannot succeed on the next tick, and retrying would be the same refusal twenty times a second for the rest of the day. The same reasoning gives the boot path its fallback: a day that cannot be drawn stands the process up on the hand-made starting floor rather than refusing to boot, because this table is one game's furniture and the landing page, the wishlist and three other games have nothing to do with it.

_And the honest limitation, because it is easy to overclaim._ `dailyLayout` is pure **with respect to a fixed binary**. The same day computed twice by the same build gives the same floor on every machine and after every deploy — but **editing the generator retroactively changes every day's office, including today's**. A deploy that touches `generate.go` therefore swaps the geometry under whoever is standing on it, with no eviction and no «РЕМОНТ», until the next 21:00 puts everybody on the new build's version of the same day. That is tolerable — a floor is furniture, the catalogue is re-served, and the client refetches on an `office_id` it does not recognise — but it is **not reproducibility**, and a day number in a log line will not bring back what an older binary drew. If that ever matters, the fix is storing the geometry rather than pinning the generator, which is the decision this record deliberately did not take.

_One consequence landed in the tests rather than in the code, and it is the kind that recurs._ The moment a production path reads the wall clock and does something destructive, **every test that drives that path against `time.Now()` acquires a once-a-day failure**. This game's service harness builds its ticks from `time.Now()` and jumps them by up to 91 seconds, so without intervention every existing service test would have crossed 21:00 once a day and read `renovated` where it asserted `left` or `promoted` — a "passes on its own, fails in CI" flake that no sanctioned mechanism could fix, because the trigger is the wall clock inside a production path rather than randomness or a race. The answer is **an in-package test edit and not a production seam**: the harness pins `installedDay` a day forward, so no instant any test can reach is a new day, and the rotation's own tests set it themselves to the boundary they are driving. A configuration flag that disabled the rotation would have been test-only machinery on a live path, which this project forbids. The suites that run the **real binary** — the full-stack Playwright suite and the testcontainers integration suite — keep a genuine exposure of a few seconds a day around 21:00 UTC; it is documented in the runbook rather than engineered away, because engineering it away means exactly the flag that is not allowed.
