---

«ВАНЯДУМ» iteration 1 shipped rung one — an authoritative server and a client that draws what it is told — deliberately, in order to measure the feel before building anything on top of it. **It was measured and it failed**: movement reads as roughly twenty frames a second, because the camera only changes when a snapshot lands, while the screen redraws sixty times a second. So the netcode was going to be built anyway. The decision recorded here is that **all four rungs are built together, now**, and that the target is not "movement feels smooth" but "a second player can be added without changing a load-bearing shape".

_Why not keep climbing one rung at a time._ The staged plan was right about one thing — do not build what you cannot yet evaluate — and wrong about the cost curve. Each rung *individually* is cheap to add later; what is expensive to retrofit is the **shape** each one requires, and those shapes are shared:

- Prediction needs a per-command sequence number and an acknowledgement. Adding one to a live protocol is a coordinated deploy.
- Interpolation needs a **timeline** — a fixed-rate tick on every snapshot and an array of entities that are not you. A tick rather than a wall-clock stamp, because the rate is agreed in the catalogue, so the number *is* the clock and the client has only to estimate where the two machines' clocks sit relative to one another. Adding an array later is another coordinated deploy, and the client has to grow a second rendering path beside the one it already has.
- Lag compensation needs the server to have been **keeping a history all along**. A ring buffer cannot be retrofitted onto the past.
- All three need the simulation to be identical on both ends, which is a claim that has to be *tested*, not asserted.

Doing them together means one protocol design, one conformance test, and one rendering path. Doing them apart means three protocol revisions and a client that grows a special case each time. That is the specific reason this is not the project's usual "build the smaller thing" answer: here the smaller things sum to more than the whole.

_What each rung is, in this game._ Prediction applies the player's own input locally through the same `Step` the server runs, keeping the commands pending; reconciliation resets to the server's authoritative position on each snapshot and replays whatever is still unacknowledged on top of it, with corrections eased in over about a tenth of a second rather than snapped. Interpolation renders everything that is *not* you a little over two snapshot periods in the past — the delay is expressed as a **multiple of that period** rather than as a duration typed out at both ends, so a change to the publish rate carries it along instead of leaving the buffer running dry — between the two authoritative snapshots that bracket that instant, because another player's intent cannot be predicted. **That instant is measured on the server's tick, never on the moment a frame arrived**, which is what binds this rung to the next: the server rewinds on the assumption that the client drew `serverTick − delay`, and an arrival timeline makes the instant actually drawn drift with the one-way delay, so a player is resolved against a moment nobody displayed. Lag compensation keeps a short ring of past world states on the server and resolves a shot against the world **as the shooter actually saw it**, rather than against the present: the **whole** staleness derived from the tick a client echoes back — never half of it, because that gap is the round trip and not a ping — plus the same interpolation delay, bounded by `RewindMax`, which is chosen in metres because what a ceiling on the rewind denies a client lying about its staleness is a distance.

_Rung four shipped without a consumer, and that was the point rather than an oversight._ For two iterations the ring recorded every tick and nothing read it: the gun arrived first and fired at nobody, and until a shot could land there was no shot to rewind. **The recorder is the one part of a netcode stack that cannot be retrofitted** — on the day you want it, the past has already happened — so it was built with everything else and left unwired, which is precisely the cost curve this record was written about. `World.resolveShot` became its first caller on 2026-08-02, and the composition it reads is the one described above, unchanged since the day it was written: this occupant's smoothed round trip plus the served interpolation delay, clamped together. Two things fell out of wiring it that the design had already implied and are worth stating where somebody will look for them. **The shooter is not rewound and neither is his aim** — his position was predicted by his own client and has just been acknowledged, and his angles arrived with the very command that pulled the trigger, so rewinding either would fire from a place he was not standing, pointing somewhere he was not looking; the sibling game reached that same split from the other end and gives the full reasoning ([ADR-059](./ADR-059-the-catch-is-resolved-in-the-victims.md)). And **a rewind is not a licence**: what a shot may *reach* is the set the shooter was actually sent, which is interest management, not lag compensation — the ring records everybody, filtered or not, and the hit test then intersects it with what was on his screen. Treating the two as one thing is how a rewind turns into a kill through a wall.

_The cost, and it is the one this design spent a record avoiding._ `Step` now exists twice — in Go and in TypeScript — which is a second implementation of one rule, exactly what the project's rules tell you not to build. It is accepted here because prediction is not possible without it, and it is made safe rather than merely permitted:

- `Step` is a pure function of `(level, player, command)` with a fixed timestep and no clock, no randomness and no query. It was written that way in iteration 1 against precisely this day.
- The two are pinned by **golden vectors**: a Go test emits `(seed, input transcript) → a trace of everything Step owns` as JSON into `testdata/`, and a vitest asserts the TypeScript reproduces it to the centimetre. The trace has grown with the function rather than staying a list of positions — the barrels, both gun timers, the spawn protection, the injection and, since the шприц, **health** — because a vector that omits a predicted field is a conformance test that cannot see that field diverge. Divergence is a red test in the ordinary gate, not a player walking through a wall somebody else can see.
- The **server remains the only authority**. Prediction is a rendering optimisation that is usually right; when it is wrong the server's answer wins, unconditionally and without negotiation. Nothing in this record moves a decision to the client, and the reconciliation path is also a free divergence audit — the server has already computed the truth, so a client that disagrees persistently is visible.
- **The second implementation is bounded to `Step`, and the hit test proves the boundary is real.** `hit.go` is pure for exactly the same reasons and is deliberately *not* in the port: a client predicts its own movement because a camera that waits for a round trip is unusable, and its own gun because a muzzle flash must run the refusal the server is about to run — but whether it hit somebody is a question about *somebody else*, and the only honest answer comes from the server. So the rule "what has to be predicted is ported, and nothing else is" has a worked case on both sides, rather than being a line nobody has had to draw. **The шприц then drew that line through a single quantity**, which is the sharpest form the rule has taken: an injection roots the man and heals him, so the *root* and the *heal* are both inside `Step` and both in the port — the browser would otherwise draw a man walking out of a room the server has him standing still in — while **damage**, the other thing that moves health, stays the world's and arrives as a correction. Health is therefore predicted in part and authoritative in whole, and the way that is kept honest is arithmetic rather than bookkeeping: what an ampoule has delivered is a pure function of the time left on it, so replaying pending commands from a snapshot lands on the server's number instead of on a sum of the client's own history.

_What "ready for multiplayer" was claimed to mean, and how the claim turned out._ It was stated precisely so it could be checked rather than believed: the arena holds several occupants and is not a room ([ADR-048](./ADR-048-the-simulation-is-a-server-owned-fixed-step.md)); the snapshot carries a peers array and a timeline, empty at the time; the simulation is deterministic and identical on both ends, with a test that says so; the server keeps a state history; and authority never moved — so adding a second player would be filling an array that already exists. **It was checked, and it held.** The building became shared and the array filled ([ADR-060](./ADR-060-there-is-one-zabroshka-nothing-ends-and-a-run.md)) without a rung being rebuilt: what changed was the *contents* of a peer entry, shrunk for bytes, which is tuning a shape rather than growing one. The history had two iterations of recorded past waiting when the first barrel went off, which is the whole of what could not have been retrofitted.

_What is deliberately still missing_, so this record does not overclaim. **Unreliable transport is the biggest structural gap**: WebSocket is TCP, so a lost packet head-of-line blocks everything behind it and a bad mobile moment produces freeze-then-jump — exactly wrong for a stream where the next snapshot supersedes the last. WebRTC DataChannel or WebTransport is the real answer and is a second transport with its own handshake, its own authentication and its own rate limiting, which is why it is not in this change. **Interest management is no longer on this list** — it arrived with the peers exactly as predicted, from the sector graph's neighbour set, and is now load-bearing rather than economical, since a shot may only reach what the shooter was sent ([ADR-060](./ADR-060-there-is-one-zabroshka-nothing-ends-and-a-run.md)). Still absent: delta compression against a baseline, binary bit-packing — which is now the named price of a **fourth** occupant or a third нейрослоп, the building having shrunk to three as the frames grew — and an adaptive jitter buffer. The шприц is worth knowing about before reaching for the codec: a rule can still be added for **zero** bytes by giving a field already on the frame a second meaning, which is cheaper than an encoding change and is bounded by how far exclusivity actually holds ([ADR-060](./ADR-060-there-is-one-zabroshka-nothing-ends-and-a-run.md)). Each is listed with its trigger in the living doc; none of them is a shape that is expensive to retrofit, which is exactly why they are not here.
