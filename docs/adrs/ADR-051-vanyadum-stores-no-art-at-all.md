«Смолтолк в Химках» keeps its pictures as blobs in Postgres ([ADR-026](./ADR-026-game-art-lives-in-postgres-not-in-git-or-the.md)) behind a shared, game-agnostic store ([ADR-031](./ADR-031-game-asset-storage-is-shared-infrastructure.md)). «ВАНЯДУМ» uses **neither**, and that is a decision rather than an omission: this game has no authored art to store. Every visible thing is a function of a seed and a handful of catalogue numbers.

- **Geometry** is extruded from the level's sector graph ([ADR-050](./ADR-050-the-level-is-generated-on-the-server-and-sent.md)). No modeller, no glTF, no loader in the bundle.
- **Textures** are generated from five short catalogue entries — a base colour, an accent, a noise amount, a roughness and a pattern name — into concrete, brick and boards.
- **Props** — the двустволка, the beer bottle and the шприц — are assembled from boxes and cylinders in about thirty lines each, and neither the shape nor the colour is chosen in the renderer: the **colour is the catalogue's `tint`** and the **shape is picked by the catalogue's own `heals` test**, the same one the server collects by, so the thing that mends you cannot be drawn as the thing you merely collect.
- **The enemy** carries no appearance on the wire at all — a нейрослоп is `{n, x, y, s}` and nothing else (`message.go`, `Foe`), and the whole of what the catalogue says about it is a Russian name and one line of blurb — and it is drawn as a **generated billboard sprite**, one typed array per facing. Which is the subject of the section below.
- **Lighting** is a per-sector level and four fixed face multipliers baked into vertex colours, drawn with an unlit material. There is no lighting pass at all, which is both cheaper and much closer to the game this is a joke about.

_The consequence on the wire, which is the point._ The entire appearance of a заброшка costs the same few kilobytes the level already costs, and **zero art bytes, forever**. Against the project's rule that bytes are a design constraint because the audience is on a phone on mobile data, this is the largest single saving available to a 3D game, and it is available only because nothing here was drawn by a person.

_The load-bearing detail: generated into a typed array, never drawn into a canvas._ A texture is a pure `(surface, size, seed) → Uint8Array` that the renderer wraps in a `DataTexture`. The obvious alternative — drawing with a 2D canvas context — produces the same pixels and is **untestable**: jsdom has no canvas, and once the pixels are on the GPU nothing can assert on them at all. As a typed array it is ordinary node-testable code, and `vanyadumTexture.spec.ts` pins that the same seed gives the same bytes, that every channel stays inside a byte, that alpha is always opaque, that a brick pattern actually contains mortar, and that an unknown pattern degrades to plain rather than throwing. This is the same move [ADR-047](./ADR-047-vanyadum-renders-in-webgl-and-only-the.md) makes with geometry, and for the same reason: a canvas can be given nothing that a test needs to look at.

_Reproducibility is a requirement, not a nicety._ The seed is explicit and the PRNG is `mulberry32` rather than `Math.random`, so a reload mid-run does not repaint the world in different concrete, and two phones looking at the same wall see the same wall. The texture seed is derived from the **surface key** rather than the level seed, deliberately: two rooms made of the same material should look like the same material.

## The enemy arrived, which was this record's own named test

The previous version of this file ended by saying that procedural art has a ceiling, that a нейрослоп is the case where that ceiling would be found, and that the enemies were the first real test of the decision. The enemy has since been built, and the outcome so far is worth stating rather than left to be inferred, because half of the test has been passed and half of it has not.

**The contract settles it hard.** A нейрослоп crosses the wire as four integers — an id, two coordinates and a room — with **no art key, no sprite id, no texture name and no appearance field of any kind**. Nothing in the shared blob store can therefore be *addressed* for one: reaching for an uploaded picture is not a matter of adding a row, it is a wire change, on a frame that repeats twenty times a second, per viewer, forever. The catalogue's `slop` object publishes a title, a blurb and its numbers — words and rules rather than pictures. So the whole appearance of the creature is the browser's to make.

**And the technique moved rather than the decision.** The creature is drawn as a **billboard sprite** rather than assembled from primitives the way the shotgun and the bottle are, which is the first thing in this game that is not a box or a cylinder — and it is still generated: `web/src/lib/vanyadumSlop.ts` is a pure `({angle, torsoHalf, seed}) → Uint8Array`, called once per facing, in `lib/` with everything else that has to be node-testable, exactly the discipline `vanyadumTexture.ts` set for walls. `torsoHalf` being an argument rather than a constant is the same rule the body model already follows: it is `player.radius / player.body_height` from the served catalogue, so the width the sprite draws is the width the server shoots at, and a creature cannot be drawn narrower than it can be hit. That the ceiling this record worried about was met by **changing the technique** rather than by fetching art is the whole outcome of the test. It is also the right technique for the joke twice over: a нейрослоп has to read as something a bad model produced — smooth, over-rendered, lit from nowhere, with too many fingers — which is a sentence a deliberately bad texture can speak and a low-poly mesh cannot, and it is the answer Doom itself gave for the same reason. A sprite is exactly where a project would normally reach for a PNG; this one did not.

**And the decision has since grown a second leg, which is harder than the first.** Originally this was an argument from taste and testability: there is no authored art, so there is nothing to store, and generating into a typed array is testable where a canvas is not. The byte budget has since made it arithmetic. The building runs with **32 B/s of headroom** against its 8 kB/s per-viewer ceiling ([ADR-060](./ADR-060-there-is-one-zabroshka-nothing-ends-and-a-run.md)), which is a byte and a half of one snapshot — so the next field of any size, on any of this game's three frames, costs an occupant a place in the building. **An art key on a слоп is not merely unnecessary now; it is unaffordable.** Note which way that dependency runs: it is the *reason* that is currently strongest, not the decision, and the codec that would free those bytes is exactly what the re-examine trigger above names.

**The second pickup kind tested the same contract from the other end, and it held there too.** The шприц is the first thing added to this building since the beer, and what the catalogue publishes about it is a Russian title, a one-line blurb, an emoji for the readouts, a hex `tint` and two numbers of rules (`internal/gamevanyadum/content.go`, `Pickups`). **No art key, no model name, no sprite id** — so, exactly as with the creature, reaching into the shared blob store for one is not a row to insert but a change to a served contract, and on the frame it would be a change to a payload with a byte and a half spare. The alternative route is worse rather than better: a bundled picture would be the **first authored asset on this route**, downloaded by everybody who opens the game whether or not they ever find one. The pattern is what to take from this rather than the instance: **the catalogue publishes what a thing IS and what it DOES, and its appearance is derived from a colour and a seed** — which is what lets a new kind of thing be one catalogue entry, and what has kept the art bytes at zero across three iterations that each added something visible.

_What this does not claim._ Procedural art is not free — it is a different kind of work, and it has a ceiling. The reason it clears this game's bar is that the target is *deliberately bad art*: a нейрослоп has to read as an over-smooth AI image with too many fingers, which is a thing a generator does convincingly and a hand would have to work at. That is the joke carrying the technique, and it is not transferable — a game wanting something genuinely drawn has the shared asset store right there, unprefixed precisely so that any game may use it. This record says this game does not need it, not that it may not have it.
